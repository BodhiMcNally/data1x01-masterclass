Prepared 3 November 2026 for Bodhi McNally's independent student society session on 10 November 2026. This is an original practice resource, not official assessment guidance.

## Authoritative scope

Canvas course 74355 is the shared DATA1001/DATA1901 Semester 2 2026 site. Its home page identifies differentiation in workshops and the group project. The current exam preparation page and current sample papers address both streams. This session covers their shared core. It makes no claim about additional advanced-stream requirements beyond the guidance actually reviewed.

The published final-exam information states: supervised, on-campus, closed book; 2 hours writing plus 10 minutes reading; no calculators; an Exam Guide is provided; 30 MCQs worth 75% and one multipart extended answer worth 25%. The current sample MCQ paper uses four options and says no negative marks. Follow the actual exam paper and personal timetable on the day. The information page says pen for MCQs, while the preparation page recommends a soft pencil. Bring both and follow the printed instructions. The sample's reading-time instruction prohibits writing during those ten minutes.

All core lecture and workshop content is assessable. Details specific to the class data stories and explicitly labelled extensions are excluded. The Week 13 recap is expressly not exhaustive. The published 29 October revision of sample MCQ Q10, rather than an older paper, was reviewed.

## Revised session

41 slides contain 13 MCQs and three short answers. There is no appendix, references slide, presenter-note content or countdown. Each content slide cites its module/topic and relevant outcomes in a visible footer. Literature appears beside the relevant advice, rather than on a separate references page.

Five additional questions cover privacy/linkage, measurement error, Binomial code, bootstrapping and goodness of fit. Short-answer rubrics assign each individual mark. The original course-based numerical and design checks still apply. The QMD is editable, and the standalone HTML works offline.

Two R Shiny widgets are embedded when the complete project is launched with launch_shiny.R. They calculate sample means and changed-null t-tests in R. The direct HTML keeps local sample-size and CI controls, so a presenter can still use it without R. All five static figures are produced by ggplot2, with an accessible palette, consistent typography and labelled units.

The final learning segment includes MCQ advice, an explicitly facetious quotation, non-exhaustive definition recall, a Notion-style error-log mockup and the official revision resources. The final slide contains Bodhi's actual photo, website QR and university email.

| 58–60 | 41 | Good luck, resources and a brief question |

Give roughly 30–45 seconds for initial MCQ thinking and use the revealed explanations to target misconceptions. The short answers need about 60–90 seconds of writing each. Core reviews can be brief if the class is comfortable. This is a delivery plan, not a classroom timing trial. No timers appear in the deck.

## Live response delivery

The slide count and pacing plan stay at 41 slides. Students join once through the session QR on the title, then follow the active question on their phones. An MCQ answer reveal locks responses and adds the A–D distribution and percentage correct to the heading, while retaining the explanations beside the original options. The small QR on every MCQ supports late arrivals. Q enlarges the joining instructions at any point. The final website QR still points to Bodhi's resources.

The voting service can run as the supplied Cloudflare Worker or as the local Node implementation. A temporary tunnel launcher is included for testing. For the event, prefer the Worker deployment over a development tunnel. The deck stays local in both cases. The offline HTML keeps its original interactions when no server is supplied. The actual R widgets can run alongside live voting.
